# Historical clock and painting study prompts

These prompts record the original visual studies. The released timer uses the painted 3D characters described in the [artwork notes](../ARTWORK.md). Some textures derived from these studies still ship; see the [asset ledger](../../ASSETS.md). The prompt wording below describes the studies at the time they were made.

## Character painting

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: single still artwork approval proof for a premium artistic classroom timer. Create ONE exquisite hand-painted fine-art illustration of a recognizable domestic shorthair cat wearing a soft vermilion artist's beret, standing in a natural three-quarter full-body pose, with its long tail lifted elegantly as if ready to paint. A glass bowl containing one vivid red goldfish sits nearby. Style: a sophisticated original oil-and-gouache painting reflecting Henri Matisse's Cat with Red Fish: confident expressive contours, rich but restrained ochre, warm cream, deep forest green, coral vermilion, muted teal; visible bristle brushwork, layered pigment, tactile linen canvas, organic asymmetry, masterful compositional balance, beautiful painterly light. This must look like a high-quality gallery painting with dimensional modeling through brushwork, NOT vector art, NOT flat icons, NOT geometric cartoon shapes, NOT a glossy 3D mascot, NOT oversize cute eyes. The cat is quietly mischievous and artistically stylized, with credible feline anatomy, graceful paws and short hair, expressive small eyes. Warm pale cream background with subtle canvas texture and breathing room around the entire cat and fish bowl; no crop, no text, no border, no watermark. One frame only, no sprite sheet, no multiple poses. Landscape composition.

## Analog frosted glass

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: ONE single still design proof for the analog scene of a classroom countdown website. Reference image 1 is the supplied Amsterdam airport clock collage: use ONLY its physical idea of a big luminous frosted-glass circular face, a blurred artist working immediately behind it, and bold hand-painted clock hands. Ignore the collage layout and all captions. Reference image 2 sets the exact rich oil/gouache painting style and identity of the domestic shorthair cat with vermilion beret. Create a beautiful sophisticated wide scene showing a single large round frosted-glass clock, filling most of the composition, with a thin dark forest-green rim against a restrained warm cream artistic background. The glass has genuine softly granular sandblasted texture, layered cloudy translucent mottling, subtle pale sage tint, fine glass grain and softly diffused illumination. Behind the glass is the soft but recognizable shadow of THAT cat in its beret, standing/rearing naturally on hind legs and reaching with an elegant raised tail to paint the long dark minute hand. Its nearby paw is erasing a faint previous brush stroke. Make the contact point between tail and hand brush stroke explicit. The painted minute hand and shorter hand are rich near-black green, with hand-painted edges, extremely readable against the luminous face, while the cat behind is softly blurred with very little visible detail. Bold brush-painted minute ticks around the perimeter; the dial is a countdown interpretation, not a literal copy of an artwork. Two hands form an open angle and remain clear. Thick enough to read across a classroom. Cat occupies the lower portion of the dial without obscuring the hand geometry. Painterly realism and physical frosted-glass depth, polished high-end art direction, not vector art and not flat UI graphics. No large central countdown numeral, no interface controls, no text, no captions, no collage, no watermarks. One still only. Preserve the quiet sophisticated Matisse-inspired palette from reference 2.

## Digital depth

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: ONE still design approval proof of a premium classroom digital countdown clock. Reference image sets the exact painterly style, ochre/cream/forest-green/vermilion palette, and identity of the beret cat. Create a sophisticated richly painted scene with a truly dimensional digital clock reading 04:58. A single four-digit clock assembly on a quiet pale warm-cream background, seen in modest three-quarter perspective from slightly above, with enough frontal readability to see all digits. Each seven-segment digit is made of substantial dark forest-green painted ceramic or wood blocks seated in deep recessed individual segment sockets. Show explicit physical front faces, long sidewalls, beveled edges, ambient occlusion, soft directional shadows, and the deep dark rear inside empty sockets. One segment is halfway sliding forward OUT of its socket towards the viewer and protrudes far enough to show its long extrusion; another segment in a different digit is halfway sliding IN from behind, with the socket tunnel and inset depth clearly readable. Keep the display readable overall. A few ejected green block fragments rest on the ground below, painted and volumetric. The SAME anatomically graceful domestic shorthair cat in a vermilion beret stands low at one side, pushing loose fragments along the ground, not touching the active digits. High-quality original oil-and-gouache painting, visible bristle strokes and rich pigment, softly modeled dimensional form, museum-quality editorial art. Consistent palette, brushwork, and cat identity from the reference. Not vector graphics, not a flat seven-segment icon, not a plastic 3D cartoon render, not a photorealistic electronics product photo. No extra animals, no decorative plants, no arrows or diagrams, no UI controls or captions, no watermark. No sprite sheet, one still frame only. Landscape composition.

## Analog tail correction

Edit this single still analog clock proof. Preserve the composition, frosted sandblasted glass, rim, painterly texture, palette, cat identity, and dark painted clock hands exactly. Make ONE correction to the cat's painting action: REMOVE the paintbrush or tool held in the raised paw, and instead make the cat's long flexible furry striped TAIL curve up naturally from the base of its back to touch the outer tip of the long minute hand in the upper right. The TAIL ITSELF is the paintbrush, with its tail tip pressed directly against the glass at the end of the long dark hand. Show the cat's two front paws lower down at the old faint erased mark, so it has four anatomically credible legs in total and one single long tail reaching to paint. No held brush, no extra limbs, no duplicate tail, no added text. Maintain diffuse shadow behind physically textured frosted glass, softly blurred cat body and a sharper tail tip where it contacts glass. Single still proof only.
